01Scope cutWeek 1

What problem does the app solve first?

A list, not a meal planner

“Nobody here plans meals. We just run out of things.”
  1. My direction

    I interviewed my flatmates: nobody plans meals, but everyone forgets what's missing at home.

  2. What the AI did

    Built a weekly meal planner with recipes that fed ingredients into a shopping list.

  3. My decision

    I cut recipes and planning and kept only the shared list. Planning can come later if anyone asks for it.

The planner solved a problem my users didn't have. The list solves the one they have every week.

02SimplificationWeek 1

How much does it cost to add something to the list?

Add items in one tap

“This should take two seconds, not a form.”
  1. My direction

    I timed myself adding five items: almost a minute, standing in front of the fridge.

  2. What the AI did

    Proposed a form with name, quantity, unit, category and store for every item.

  3. My decision

    I replaced the form with a single text field that understands "oat milk x2" and keeps recent items one tap away.

If adding an item takes longer than remembering it, nobody adds it.

03Real behaviorWeek 2

What do flatmates need to know about each item?

Who asked and who bought

“The fights aren't about the milk, they're about who always buys it.”
  1. My direction

    I noticed the real friction wasn't the list: it was not knowing who bought what, and who owed whom.

  2. What the AI did

    Showed a clean list where every item looked the same, with no owner.

  3. My decision

    I added a colored avatar for who asked for each item and a "bought by" mark when it's checked off.

In a shared home, who bought it matters as much as what was bought.

04Visual judgmentWeek 3

How should a shared-home app feel?

A home, not a spreadsheet

“It looks like a to-do app for work. This is our kitchen.”
  1. My direction

    I gathered references from home and kitchen products and asked for something warm and calm, far from a productivity tool.

  2. What the AI did

    Proposed a high-contrast black and white theme with a dense layout.

  3. My decision

    I chose a warm paper tone with a deep green accent and more air between items.

It's used at home, often in a hurry: it should feel like part of the kitchen, not like work.

05LaunchWeek 3

When should the app interrupt people?

One summary instead of every ping

“I got eleven notifications for one grocery run.”
  1. My direction

    Before sharing it with more homes, I tested it for a week with notifications on.

  2. What the AI did

    Sent a push notification every time anyone added or checked off an item.

  3. My decision

    I replaced them with a single daily summary at 6 pm, before people head home.

An app that pings for every banana gets muted, and then the list stops working.
